RepoPrompt moved from the paid Classic product to a free, open-source Community Edition. The founder's [transition announcement](/blog/repo-prompt-next-chapter/) states that Classic's licensing restrictions were removed in the **2.1.32 update** and that all then-current subscriptions were cancelled.

Community Edition itself does not require an old Classic license or a new RepoPrompt subscription.

## What should I install now?

For the current community application, follow [Installation](../overview/installation.mdx). The [Community Edition launch announcement](/blog/repo-prompt-community-edition/) links the active RepoPrompt CE repository and the archived Classic source and download.

If you already use Classic, follow [How Do I Safely Migrate from Classic to RepoPrompt CE?](./migrate-from-classic.mdx) before replacing your setup.

Classic remains an archived product, not the current RepoPrompt CE interface. The documentation label **Classic 2.1.0** identifies the archive; it is not a claim that Classic stopped at that application version. Use the documentation appropriate to the app you installed.

## Does RepoPrompt include every feature from the paid app?

Do not assume feature-for-feature equivalence. RepoPrompt CE concentrates on context engineering and coding-agent workflows; the launch announcement describes the removal of legacy features. Consult current guides for a specific workflow rather than treating an old purchase, screenshot, or tutorial as a current feature list.

## Why might I still pay for AI usage?

Removing RepoPrompt's application subscription did not make external services free. Coding-runtime plans, API usage, and other services have their own terms and billing. See [Do I Need to Pay for AI Services?](./pay-for-services.mdx) and the [provider directory](../providers/overview.mdx) for those account boundaries.

For current participation and support, see [Who Maintains RepoPrompt?](./solo-project.mdx).
